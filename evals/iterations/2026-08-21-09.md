## The round in one sentence

The half point that had stood since iteration 04 finally moved — and the requirement that moved it was defective in exactly the way this project has been failing for six rounds.

`003` FR-032 opens an **Unavailability** when a machine stops being available to work. Three agents found, independently, that **nothing ever closed one.**

## Julia's deduction, which is the finding of the round

She grepped `lift|resum|available again|reinstate|cleared|released` across `003` and `Unavailab` across all three specs. Nothing lifts a Safety Stop. Nothing records an Incident as no longer stopping work. FR-019 said the Unavailability runs *"for as long as the stop holds"* and no requirement ever ended the holding.

> *"A two-day safety stop opens an Unavailability that never closes; `002` FR-031 then answers 'yes, Lea$e's machine was standing' for **every** milestone of that project from that day forward. One exercise of my single unconditional `May` permanently converts every later slip of that client's project into Lea$e's fault, in the hands of the man who decides defaults, with no act available to me to retract it."*

And on whether that pressures her not to stop a machine — the question she was asked to test:

> *"No requirement gates my stop, but an unclosable record does the same work informally: it makes the safety stop the most expensive act in the system, and keeps it expensive forever. My file's posture is that safety is the one ground that never depends on the state of the contract."*

Her second leg: FR-012 opened an Unavailability for a Service Window passing uncompleted, while this spec's own Key Product Decisions say that below BR-10's threshold *"nothing forces the machine to stop"* and *"a client who postpones is not thereby in breach"*.

> *"Postponing now costs him **less** than nothing: it buys him an exculpatory record with the underwriter, generated out of my service file, while his machine ran two shifts a day."*
